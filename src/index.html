Sync Between Devices
File route — no clipboard. Save backup file writes a .json (into Downloads on the phone). On the other device: Import from file, pick it, then Load imported data twice to confirm.
Paste route: Export data → copy the box → Import data on the other device → paste → Load twice. Fragile on a phone: long payloads get truncated by the clipboard.

Where these numbers come from
Body fat · US Navy girth method
Estimated from body measurements, not measured directly. Male: 495 ÷ (1.0324 − 0.19077 × log₁₀(waist − neck) + 0.15456 × log₁₀(height)) − 450. Female: 495 ÷ (1.29579 − 0.35004 × log₁₀(waist + hips − neck) + 0.22100 × log₁₀(height)) − 450. All girths in centimetres. Published by Hodgdon and Beckett (1984) for the US Navy. Typical agreement with a DEXA scan is around ±3–4 percentage points, and it is more reliable for tracking change over time than for any single reading. Nothing is shown until every measurement the formula needs has been logged.
Starting energy estimate · Mifflin-St Jeor
Resting rate = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age, then +5 for men or −161 for women. Multiplied by an activity factor of 1.2 to 1.9 depending on how much you train. Mifflin-St Jeor (1990) is the equation most dietetic practice uses, and is a population average: individual resting rates vary by roughly ±10% around it, more if you are very lean, very heavy, or older.
Adaptive Maintenance Estimate
Your calorie maintenance estimate, continuously calibrated using your actual food logs and weight trend. Once there are about two weeks of data, the app stops relying on a formula and works from your own numbers. It fits a straight line through your weigh-ins to get a trend in kg per day, and compares it with your average logged intake over the same span: maintenance = average intake − (trend × 7,700). The 7,700 kcal per kg figure is the standard approximation for body tissue. It is only an approximation: early weight change is largely water and glycogen, which carry almost no energy, so short windows overstate or understate badly. That is why 14 days is the minimum and 28 is preferred, and why the app flags a window it does not trust. 91 days (13 weeks) smooths out the most, but if your weight has changed direction within it, the answer lags behind where you are now. Your logging does not have to be accurate, only consistent. Because the estimate compares what you log with what your weight actually does, a habit of logging a food low or high is built into the maintenance figure, and the target that comes out of it works in the same numbers. What throws it is a change: starting to weigh food, counting something you used to leave out, or skipping whole meals on some days and not others. After a change like that, give it a couple of weeks.
Cardio and calories
Tally doesn’t estimate calories burned. Machines and watches often overestimate exercise burn, and adding those numbers back to your target can quietly undo a deficit. You don’t need them: your adaptive maintenance estimate is worked out from what you eat and what your weight actually does, so the energy your cardio burns is already in it. Do more cardio and the estimate rises to match within a couple of weeks; do less and it falls. If your cardio changes a lot from week to week, the estimate lags behind by a week or two.
Daily target · your goal rate
Target = maintenance + (goal kg per week × 7,700 ÷ 7). A goal of −0.5 kg per week is a deficit of about 550 kcal a day. The app will not suggest below 1,500 kcal for men or 1,200 for women, whatever the arithmetic produces, because eating adequately below that is difficult without supervision. The dot next to Energy compares your average with the target: green within a tenth either way, yellow when lower than that, red when more than a tenth over. It is also red under 1,500 kcal a day for men or 1,200 for women (1,200 when sex is not set), whatever your target.
Protein · one number, no maths
100 g a day for women, 140 g for men. Research puts the point where extra protein stops helping muscle at about 1.6 g per kg of a healthy body weight. For a woman up to about 175 cm that comes to 100 g or less, and for a man up to about 190 cm, 140 g or less, so one number covers nearly everyone who is not a professional athlete. Body fat needs no protein, so carrying more of it does not raise the number.

Anything from three quarters of it is fine. That matches the low end of current guidelines (1.2 g per kg). Muscle responds to what you eat over weeks, not on any single day, so a low day now and then costs nothing.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding raise the need. Set your own number then, or whenever you have been given one.
Next-session suggestions · patient double progression
Each movement can carry a rep range: pick Power (3–6 reps), Muscle (6–8) or Endurance (15–20), or set your own. The coach reads your last session of that movement at its heaviest load, so warm-up sets never count.

Reps first, then load, and one target for every set. At the same weight it asks for one more rep than your weakest set managed, on every set, within the range: last time 7, 6, 7 means at least 7 on all three next time. Straight sets at one weight are prescribed as one number, and the weakest set is exactly what stands between you and the next weight. A set that beats the target is ahead of it, not asked for less. Once every working set reaches the top in two sessions running, it adds the weight step and the target starts again from the bottom. One clean session earns a second go at the same weight rather than a heavier bar: one good day is as often a good night's sleep as real progress.

A set below the bottom of the range keeps the weight and makes the bottom the target. Holds climb five seconds at a time. A step of zero means reps only, and at the top it suggests raising the range.

The suggestions are placeholders. What you type is what is recorded, and the advice itself is never stored: it is read off your history every time, so correcting a past session changes it.
Records · estimated one-rep max
Records are read off your history every time and never stored, so a corrected or deleted day cannot leave a stale best behind. Loaded lifts keep the heaviest set and the best estimated one-rep max; bodyweight lifts keep the most reps and the heaviest added weight; holds keep the longest.

Estimated one-rep max uses Epley: weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30), and only for sets of 1 to 12 reps. Past about twelve the formula drifts badly, so a set of twenty says little about a single: endurance-range sets still count towards the heaviest set, just not towards the estimate. A single counts as its own weight.

A first session sets no records, because there is nothing yet to beat.
Age and activity
Age is entered directly rather than as a year of birth, so there is no guessing whether a birthday has passed. It is stored with the date you set it and advances on its own, so the estimate does not quietly go stale — correct it any time.

Activity multipliers run 1.2 to 1.9. Every app describes these as "1–3 sessions a week" without ever saying what a session is, so this one gives hours of actual working time and judges effort by whether you can still talk — full conversation is easy, short sentences is moderate, barely a word is hard. Count your job and daily life too, not just the gym. If you are between two levels take the lower one: overshooting here is the commonest mistake, and it only affects the starting estimate anyway.
These are estimates for tracking your own training, not medical or dietary advice. Every formula here is a population average applied to one person, and every one of them can be wrong for you. Nothing in this app is a diagnosis, a treatment, or a substitute for a doctor or a registered dietitian. If you are pregnant, under 18, managing a medical condition or an eating disorder, or taking medication affected by diet, speak to a qualified professional before acting on any number here.
